About CADC

CADC (computer-aided detection and characterisation) is a student research project exploring how 3D deep learning can help describe lung nodules on CT.

Medical disclaimer

CADC is an artificial-intelligence research prototype. It is not a medical device, has not been reviewed or approved by any regulator, and has not been clinically validated.

Its output is an automated estimate of how radiologists in one research dataset might rate a nodule's appearance. It cannot diagnose or rule out cancer or any other disease, and it can be wrong.

Always seek the advice of a qualified doctor about any medical question, scan or symptom. Never disregard or delay professional advice because of something shown here. In an emergency, contact your local emergency number (for example 112 in India and Europe, 911 in the US).

Privacy

Your scans stay on this computer. The analyser runs locally: uploaded files are processed by a program on your own machine, kept in memory only while you use them, and never sent to an external server.

The only internet requests are for web fonts and, if you click "Try a sample scan", a one-time download of a public research scan from The Cancer Imaging Archive.

DICOM files can contain personal information such as names and dates of birth. The app does not display or store it, but take care when sharing scans or reports.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can this tell me whether I have lung cancer?

No. Only a doctor can diagnose cancer, usually with a biopsy. This model estimates how suspicious a nodule looks to radiologists in a research dataset. A high score does not mean cancer, and a low score does not rule it out.

What does the percentage mean?

It is the average of five models' estimates that radiologists would rate the nodule as likely malignant (an average rating of 3.5 or more out of 5). It is a model score, not a medical probability for you. Below 30% is shown as low suspicion, 30–70% intermediate, and above 70% high.

How accurate is it?

On patients it never saw, it separates nodules that radiologists rated malignant from benign ones with an AUC of about 0.92 and about 87% accuracy. The model page has the full results and limitations. Real-world accuracy on other hospitals' scans is likely to be lower.

What is the orange "AI attention" heatmap?

A Grad-CAM map: it highlights the parts of the 3D cube that most pushed the models towards "malignant". It shows where the model looked, not proof of disease, and it is sometimes drawn to surrounding structures rather than the nodule itself.

Why do I have to click on the nodule myself?

This model classifies nodules; it does not search for them. Finding nodules automatically needs a separate detection model, which is a possible next step for this project.

I don't have a CT scan. Where can I get one to try?

Click Try a sample scan on the Analyse page for a quick demo (that scan was in the training data). For scans the model has never seen, run python -m cadc.get_samples in the project folder. It downloads three free public scans from The Cancer Imaging Archive (one confirmed lung cancer, one confirmed benign, one adenocarcinoma) as .zip files in data\samples, ready to upload.

What kind of scan can I upload?

A chest CT scan in DICOM format: a .zip of one series, or all the .dcm files of that series. Thin slices (1–2.5 mm) work best. X-rays, MRI, photos and PDFs will not work.

Why does it say my LIDC-IDRI scan is in the training data?

The models learned from the LIDC-IDRI dataset, so scores on those scans are optimistic. Honest performance figures, measured only on unseen patients, are on the model page.

I'm worried about my lungs. What should I do?

Please talk to a doctor. The when to see a doctor section lists symptoms that should be checked, and when to seek emergency help.

Built with

PyTorch (3D ResNet, trained on a laptop GPU) · pylidc and pydicom (data) · FastAPI (local server) · Three.js (3D lung model) · hand-written SVG charts. Source code: github.com/Dhir-learner/CAD-C.
